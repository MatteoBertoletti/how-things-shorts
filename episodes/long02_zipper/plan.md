## Fact sheet (checked 2026-11-13; sources at the bottom; DROPPED items listed)
| # | Fact (as it will be said / shown) | Sources | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A zipper is two rows of teeth on two fabric tapes plus a slider; inside the slider a Y-shaped channel meshes the rows when it moves one way and separates them when it moves the other | S1, S2, S3, S12 | core idea |
| 2 | The teeth of one row alternate with the teeth of the other: the rows are shifted along the chain, so each tooth sits between two teeth of the other row (= half a tooth spacing) | S1 ("teeth on one side alternate with those on the other"), S4 (rows "offset in the longitudinal direction"), S2 | "half a tooth" = half of the tooth spacing, a direct consequence of equal spacing + alternation; said as "half a tooth" |
| 3 | Each tooth has a bump on top (nib) and a hollow underneath (scoop); the bump of one tooth fits into the hollow of the next | S2 (nib / scoop), S1 (projection + recess), S5 (1917 patent fig. 9) | |
| 4 | Why it holds: each tooth head is wider than the opening it sits in, the teeth are packed tight on tapes that do not stretch, so a straight sideways pull cannot slide them apart; the locked fit resists pulls across and along the chain | S2, S1 ("snug fit", stable against transverse and longitudinal forces) | no strength numbers (none verified) |
| 5 | Pushing two rows straight together does not work (the wide heads cannot pass the narrow openings): they jam | S2 | |
| 6 | The Y tilts the teeth / bends the chain slightly, just enough for each tooth to clear the one ahead and drop into place | S2 ("tilts the teeth at just the right angle"), S3 ("bends the zipper slightly to open up the space") | |
| 7 | Going down, a wedge-shaped divider inside the slider pushes the teeth apart; because it must stay between the teeth, no zipper is ever fully closed at the top | S2, S12 (divider) | EP. 01 twist, reused once |
| 8 | Stops: top stops keep the slider from running off the end; a bottom stop holds the two rows together at the bottom | S3 | |
| 9 | Pull tab: it is the handle; on many sliders it also works a small lock: when the tab lies flat (no pull) a pin/pawl drops between two teeth and the slider stays put; pulling the tab lifts it | S10, S8, S11 | "many sliders", no percentage |
| 10 | Sizes: the size number is roughly the width of the closed chain in millimetres: size 5 = about 5 mm | S6, S7, S1 (gauges #3-#10) | on screen ABOUT 5 mm |
| 11 | Coil: a plastic (usually polyester) thread wound into a coil and sewn (or woven) onto the tape; the most common kind today | S1 ("bulk of worldwide sales"), S14 (coil sewn into the tape), S2 ("most popular") | |
| 12 | Molded plastic: plastic teeth (usually polyacetal) molded directly onto the edge of the tape | S1 (polyacetal), S14, S15 | |
| 13 | Metal: separate metal teeth (brass, aluminium, nickel), cut from wire, stamped with the bump and hollow, and clamped onto the tape edge | S1, S2 | |
| 14 | 1893: a Chicago inventor patents a "clasp locker", a hook-and-eye fastener for shoes (patent 504,038, 29 Aug 1893), shown at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair; it was not a commercial success | S1, S16, S17, S18 | person's name optional (W. L. Judson) |
| 15 | 1913: G. Sundback replaces the hooks with interlocking teeth (two facing rows pulled together by a slider); patent "Separable Fastener" granted 20 March 1917 (US 1,219,881, filed 1914/1915) | S1, S19, S20, S5 | filing dates disagree (1914 on the drawing, 1915 per the museum) -> say only 1917 for the patent |
| 16 | 1923: a maker of rubber boots called its boots "zippers" after the zip sound, and the name moved to the fastener | S1, S21, S22, S23 | the company is never named |
| 17 | Failure: lose one tooth and its neighbours lose their hold, the chain opens behind the gap | S2, S1 | EP. 01 |
| 18 | Failure: a worn or spread slider no longer presses the teeth together, the rows split right behind it | S1, S24, S25 | |
| 19 | Repair tip (verified): squeeze the slider plates very gently with pliers to close the gap again; not too hard or it jams | S1, S2, S24, S25 | kept |
| 20 | Repair tip (verified, optional): a stuck slider: rub a pencil tip on the teeth, graphite is a dry lubricant | S2, S27, S28 | callback to LONG 01 (one tag) |

## NEW 3D shots (5, everything else re-framed from EP. 01 js/zipper.js parts)
1. X-ray slider: the slider body in blue-white glass (fresnel rim, transmission look), the Y channel and the tooth path glowing white-blue where teeth pass, the divider turning yellow when it works. Used in the hook, CH. 3 and CH. 4.
2. Coil chain: a plastic thread winding into a spiral along the tape edge, then meshing (new geometry in js/zipper.js: `coilChain`).
3. Molded-plastic chain: chunky, symmetric matte teeth sitting on the tape edge (new tooth profile, same Chain layout).
4. Lock cutaway: the pull tab lying flat drops a small pin between two teeth; lifting the tab raises it (slider section).
5. Worn slider: the mouth spread a fraction too wide, the rows close inside and slip apart just behind it.
Extensions to js/zipper.js / js/physics.js (not new shots): longer chains for 16:9, the tilt of the teeth inside the Y as a function of position, the wedge glow, a "jam" layout (two rows pushed straight together), the tooth pop reused.
