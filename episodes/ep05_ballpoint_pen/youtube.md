## Description
The tiny ball in a pen's tip does two jobs at once: it caps the ink so it can't dry out, and it rolls the thick ink onto the paper. Then a pen is made: injection molded barrel, the tip closed around the ball, ink injected, assembled, in under five minutes.

Notes: the ball is usually tungsten carbide (also brass or steel), held in a brass socket, which is why the on-screen label says USUALLY. The ink is a thick oil-based paste; the rolling ball picks it up from the reservoir and lays it on the paper, and the same ball seals the tube when the pen is still. "Under five minutes" from raw materials to finished pen applies to advanced factories (encyclopedia.com), and the narration says so. The ball diameter (about 0.5-1.0 mm) is not shown on screen: the cutaway is enlarged and the ENLARGED tag stays on while it is. The 1888 rolling-ball patent was left out (no room without a list).
